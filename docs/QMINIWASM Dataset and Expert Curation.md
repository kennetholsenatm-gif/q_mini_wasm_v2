# **QMINIWASM Architecture: Foundational Datasets and Leading Domain Experts for Extreme-Edge AI**

## **The Thermodynamic and Computational Imperative for Extreme-Edge Artificial Intelligence**

The deployment of large-scale artificial intelligence models at the extreme edge represents one of the most significant engineering challenges in contemporary computer science. Traditional neural network architectures, heavily reliant on continuous, high-precision floating-point arithmetic (FP16 or BF16), are fundamentally bottlenecked by the von Neumann architecture. In edge environments—where power constraints, memory bandwidth, and thermal dissipation are rigidly bound—the energy cost of fetching floating-point weights from memory far exceeds the cost of the computation itself. The QMINIWASM architecture is conceptualized as a radical departure from this paradigm. It completely eradicates continuous floating-point arithmetic, synthesizing 1.58-bit ternary quantization, hybrid quantum-classical computing, and WebAssembly (WASM) execution into a unified, highly efficient framework.

By restricting neural network parameters to a ternary lattice of ![][image1], QMINIWASM achieves an information density of exactly ![][image2] bits per parameter.1 This extreme quantization allows the architecture to replace computationally expensive floating-point matrix multiplications with highly optimized, low-cost integer additions.1 When compiled to WebAssembly, this multiplier-less architecture enables near-native, cross-platform execution within highly constrained, sandboxed environments without sacrificing deterministic performance.3 However, training a network to operate within this discrete ternary space without suffering catastrophic capacity collapse requires a precise, four-phase "Cascade Reinforcement Learning" (Cascade RL) curriculum.

The Cascade RL pipeline systematically transitions the model from a continuous parametric space into a frozen discrete lattice. Phase 1 (Continuous Supervised Fine-Tuning) maps the global topological boundaries of the target domains. Phase 2 (Progressive Quantization) employs simulated annealing to freeze the continuous sub-graphs into the ternary lattice. Phase 3 (Multi-Domain On-Policy Distillation, or MOPD) injects massive volumes of synthetic reasoning traces into the student model to preserve complex decision boundaries despite the extreme compression. Finally, Phase 4 (Geometric Routing via CISPO) trains a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) router using Clipped Importance Sampling Policy Optimization to navigate the non-differentiable tropical geometry of the ternary space.

The successful materialization of this architecture is entirely dependent upon two foundational pillars: the curation of hyper-specific dataset distributions to stabilize the Cascade RL curriculum, and the integration of advanced theoretical frameworks pioneered by leading domain experts in discrete optimization, hypersimplex topology, and quantum information theory.

## **Task 1: Hugging Face Dataset Curation and Distributional Analysis**

The transition from a continuous, full-precision loss landscape to a discrete ternary space is an inherently violent mathematical operation. During Phase 2, as simulated annealing forces weights to snap to ![][image1], the network is prone to severe representation collapse. To mitigate this, the training distributions must be meticulously curated to establish rigid, highly redundant, and deterministic decision boundaries. The following datasets, sourced from the Hugging Face repository, fulfill the strict architectural prerequisites required for the QMINIWASM Cascade RL pipeline.

### **Domain A: STEM and Logical Reasoning Datasets**

The objective of the continuous SFT phase (Phase 1\) is to sculpt deep, highly stable basins in the continuous loss landscape before quantization begins. STEM and logic datasets are uniquely suited for this task because mathematical reasoning enforces a strict sequence of deterministic state transitions. If the continuous model learns a brittle, single-path logic, the subsequent quantization noise will shatter the reasoning chain. Therefore, the datasets curated for this domain must provide dense, scaffolded, and multi-path reasoning trajectories.

The OpenMathInstruct-2 dataset constitutes a massive expansion of the original OpenMathInstruct-1 corpus, comprising 14 million problem-solution pairs generated through sophisticated code-interpreter synthesis pipelines.5 The integration of this dataset is absolutely critical for the initial conditioning of the QMINIWASM architecture. During Phase 1, the continuous teacher model must learn to navigate complex logical spaces without relying on superficial heuristics. OpenMathInstruct-2 includes highly structured instruction sequences and scaffolded hint systems that systematically guide the network through verifiable logical deductions.7 This scaffolding physically alters the geometry of the network's continuous representations, carving wide minima in the topological manifold. Consequently, when Phase 2 simulated annealing begins to discretize the weights, the broad loss basins ensure that the discrete jumps do not eject the model's logic from a viable solution state. The dataset's scale—14 million pairs—ensures that the global topological boundaries mapped during Phase 1 are robust enough to survive the entropy of quantization.

Complementing this foundational scaffolding is OpenR1-Math-220k, a specialized dataset offering 220,000 mathematical reasoning examples at scale.9 The defining characteristic of this dataset that justifies its inclusion in the QMINIWASM pipeline is its explicit provision of multiple solution traces for identical mathematical problems.9 In the context of mapping a network for eventual ternary deployment, single-path reasoning is a fatal vulnerability. If a continuous network only knows one algebraic method to solve a problem, the quantization process might destroy the specific sub-graph responsible for that method, rendering the model incapable of solving the task. By exposing the network to OpenR1-Math-220k during continuous SFT, the model is forced to map redundant reasoning pathways—such as combining algebraic manipulation with geometric approximations for the same problem. This entanglement guarantees that if one mathematical pathway is degraded during the discretization process, alternative pathways mapped within the ternary lattice remain fully functional, thus preserving the model's overall reasoning capability at the extreme edge.

### **Domain B: Competitive Code Generation Datasets**

While Domain A establishes the abstract logical reasoning capabilities of the model, Domain B serves to ground that logic in executable, deterministic reality. Execution-based coding datasets enforce a binary reward signal: the generated code either compiles and passes unit tests, or it fails. This strict feedback mechanism is essential for bridging the gap between continuous abstraction and discrete execution, particularly because QMINIWASM relies on WebAssembly for its runtime environment.

The xCodeEval dataset is a monumental compilation of test-case based multilingual code execution data, spanning seven distinct programming evaluation tasks, including code compilation, program synthesis, and automatic program repair.10 For the QMINIWASM architecture, the value of xCodeEval lies strictly in its execution-based evaluation structure. Standard autoregressive language models often suffer from logical drift; they generate code that appears syntactically plausible to human reviewers but contains fatal semantic errors that prevent execution.13 In a 1.58-bit ternary space, hallucinatory manifolds lead to cascading execution failures. By training the Phase 1 continuous model on the xCodeEval distribution, the network learns to optimize for functional equivalence and strict adherence to Abstract Syntax Trees (ASTs) rather than mere token-matching. As the architecture undergoes Phase 2 quantization, the structural rigidity enforced by xCodeEval acts as a powerful mathematical regularizer. It ensures that the surviving discrete sub-graphs naturally favor valid syntax topologies. Furthermore, because QMINIWASM utilizes WebAssembly—a low-level binary instruction format 3—learning from a dataset that supports multi-lingual, low-level execution semantics is a non-negotiable prerequisite for generating highly optimized WASM bytecode.

Similarly, the coding-eval dataset provides highly complex, practical project generation tasks that span frontend application development, framework conversion, SVG generation, and static page development.14 Its inclusion is justified by its explicit focus on the types of computational tasks most frequently encountered in extreme-edge deployments. QMINIWASM is designed to operate autonomously in constrained edge environments (such as routers, IoT devices, or embedded systems). The inference traces provided in the coding-eval repository allow the architecture to map high-level user intents directly into low-overhead executables.14 By mastering these practical edge-deployment scenarios during the continuous phase, the model ensures that its eventual quantized state is highly specialized for the exact environments where WebAssembly runtimes dominate.

### **Domain C: Synthetic Reasoning Traces for MOPD Distillation**

Phase 3 of the Cascade RL curriculum requires Multi-Domain On-Policy Distillation (MOPD). In this highly sensitive phase, the capabilities of the continuous teacher checkpoints are distilled into the 1.58-bit student model. Because a ternary model has exponentially less continuous representational capacity than a full-precision model, standard cross-entropy knowledge distillation is insufficient to transfer complex capabilities. The ternary student requires vast, unbroken sequences of dense Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories to prevent capacity collapse.

The AM-DeepSeek-R1-Distilled-1.4M dataset serves as the primary engine for the Phase 3 MOPD process. Comprising 1.4 million high-quality reasoning traces distilled predominantly from the DeepSeek-R1 architecture, this dataset captures explicit, step-by-step reasoning processes enclosed within specialized \<think\> and \</think\> boundaries.15 In the context of training 1.58-bit LLMs, standard evaluation loss over final answers fails to guide the discrete weights into a functional configuration, as the extreme quantization introduces catastrophic variance in the loss gradients. However, by leveraging the step-by-step thinking traces of AM-DeepSeek-R1-Distilled, the MOPD algorithm can apply token-level Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalties across long temporal horizons. A critical feature of this dataset is that its output traces are frequently 1.5 to 2 times longer than earlier generations of synthetic data, reflecting explicit, highly detailed reasoning.17 This sheer length acts as a dense supervisory signal, forcefully mapping the intermediate state transitions of the continuous teacher model directly into the rigid ternary lattice of the QMINIWASM student.

To supplement this process, the SSR-CoT-16k dataset provides a highly unique distributional property required for extreme-edge robustness: Distilled Structural Skeleton-guided Reasoning (SSR-D).18 This dataset specializes in converting high-quality, answer-only pairs into structured reverse-CoT training samples.18 During the MOPD phase, it is mathematically inevitable that the 1.58-bit student will encounter domains where deep reasoning traces are absent from the primary training corpus, threatening a sudden drop in policy optimization. SSR-CoT-16k provides functional tags and structural skeletons that teach the ternary model how to construct and hallucinate its own reasoning pathways when confronted with sparse data environments.18 This guarantees that the geometric routing mechanisms built in Phase 4 have continuous, unbroken chains of thought to traverse, ensuring stability across the entire hypersimplex topology.

Finally, the inclusion of OpenThoughts-114k provides an additional layer of regularization. Recognized as a flagship dataset for open reasoning that has successfully trained over 230 models 9, its 114,000 dense reasoning traces ensure that the baseline distributions of the teacher model are sufficiently broad. By utilizing OpenThoughts-114k as a distillation anchor, the QMINIWASM architecture ensures that its fundamental reasoning capabilities remain aligned with general-purpose AI instructions before specializing in complex edge-execution behaviors.

The defining physical characteristic of the QMINIWASM framework is its complete eradication of floating-point arithmetic. Moving from continuous variables to a discrete ternary space of ![][image1] necessitates a foundational redesign of traditional neural network matrix multiplications, substituting them entirely with low-cost integer additions.

Shuming Ma, a lead researcher at Microsoft Research, is recognized as the foundational architect of the 1.58-bit LLM paradigm.1 The groundbreaking research surrounding BitNet b1.58 demonstrated that large language models can match the perplexity and downstream task performance of full-precision 16-bit models while restricting every single parameter to a ternary value.19 Ma's methodology completely abandons standard post-training quantization (PTQ), which is widely known to cause severe accuracy degradation and manifold collapse at extreme low-bit widths. For the QMINIWASM architecture, Ma's theoretical framework is indispensable during Phase 2 (Progressive Quantization). Ma's techniques for natively scaling 1.58-bit models provide the exact mathematical foundation required to bound quantization error during simulated annealing. By gradually freezing sub-graphs into the ternary lattice using the principles derived from the BitNet architecture, QMINIWASM can successfully execute the phase transition. Furthermore, because QMINIWASM relies heavily on WebAssembly for edge execution, the ability to completely bypass hardware-level floating-point multiply-accumulate (MAC) units in favor of pure integer addition—a direct physical result of Ma's ternary architecture 2—is the primary mechanism that allows the framework to operate efficiently on the universally constrained hardware typical of WebAssembly deployments.

### **Pillar 2: Discrete Reinforcement Learning and Variance Bounding**

Reinforcement learning over discrete, highly quantized action spaces is notoriously unstable. High variance in advantage estimation frequently leads to catastrophic policy degradation. Phase 4 of QMINIWASM requires navigating a non-differentiable topology, mandating an RL algorithm that retains learning signals for every single token without destabilizing the trajectory.

Binyang Jiang, alongside the core research team at MiniMax AI, engineered the Clipped Importance Sampling Policy Optimization (CISPO) algorithm, an innovation critical to the MiniMax-M1 architecture.23 CISPO introduces a paradigm shift in reinforcement learning applied to autoregressive sequence generation: instead of clipping token updates as is standard in Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), CISPO clips the token-level importance sampling (IS) weights themselves.23 By abandoning the traditional trust region constraint in favor of this novel clipping strategy, the algorithm ensures that the variance of the off-policy REINFORCE objective is tightly bounded while crucially retaining the learning signal for all tokens in a sequence.23

In Phase 4 of the QMINIWASM curriculum, the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) router must make discrete, non-differentiable routing decisions within a highly fragile ternary space. Traditional PPO frequently drops tokens with low base probabilities but high subsequent rewards (the critical "Aha" or "Wait" tokens vital for maintaining structural integrity in Chain-of-Thought reasoning).23 Jiang's CISPO framework is uniquely qualified to optimize the QMINIWASM router. By clipping the importance sampling weights at ![][image3] (e.g., 0.3), the algorithm prevents the variance spikes that would otherwise shatter the 1.58-bit routing lattice, allowing the MoE system to converge significantly faster and achieve higher stability over long-horizon mathematical and execution tasks.23

Simultaneously, the theoretical work of Wei Ping at NVIDIA on Nemotron-Cascade 2 provides the exact blueprint for Phase 3\.29 Ping's research details the execution of Cascade RL and Multi-Domain On-Policy Distillation (MOPD) in compact models.33 Ping demonstrated that distilling capabilities from the strongest intermediate teacher models on-policy during the RL process allows for the efficient recovery of benchmark regressions that naturally occur during extreme scaling or quantization events.29 For QMINIWASM, Ping's formulation of MOPD is exactly the mechanism required to prevent capacity collapse when the model is abruptly frozen into a 1.58-bit state. Ping's hyperparameter scaling laws for multi-domain reinforcement learning ensure that the dense token-level supervision derived from synthetic datasets (such as AM-DeepSeek-R1-Distilled) successfully transfers into the highly compressed, quantized student model without loss of reasoning fidelity.

The routing mechanism in a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture typically relies on continuous mathematical heuristics, such as softmax gating functions, to distribute tokens to appropriate experts. However, QMINIWASM operates in a fully discrete ternary space, meaning traditional continuous gating networks involving exponents and divisions are fundamentally incompatible with the architecture's constraints.

Ye Su and Yong Liu have conducted groundbreaking, highly theoretical research analyzing Mixture-of-Experts architectures strictly through the lens of tropical geometry.34 Their collaborative research established the very first rigorous mathematical proof that the standard Top\-![][image4] routing mechanism used in MoE models is not merely a heuristic, but is algebraically isomorphic to the ![][image4]\-th elementary symmetric tropical polynomial.34 This profound isomorphism demonstrates that the routing mechanism physically partitions the high-dimensional input space into the Normal Fan of a Hypersimplex.34

This theoretical breakthrough serves as the central linchpin for QMINIWASM's Phase 4 (Geometric Routing via CISPO). Because QMINIWASM is mandated to completely eliminate continuous floating-point arithmetic to maintain its edge-deployment efficiency, the MoE router must operate using discrete combinatorial depth. Su and Liu mathematically proved that sparsity in MoE architectures is functionally equivalent to combinatorial depth, scaling the geometric capacity of the network by the binomial coefficient ![][image5].34 Furthermore, their rigorous formulation of the "Coherence Barrier" provides exact mathematical proofs indicating that when expert representations exhibit high mutual coherence, greedy routing strategies theoretically fail to recover the optimal expert subset.35

By actively applying Su and Liu's tropical geometry framework, the QMINIWASM MoE router can map incoming 1.58-bit token representations directly into the affine Voronoi diagrams of the hypersimplex. In tropical geometry (specifically the max-plus semiring), standard addition is replaced by the maximum operation, and standard multiplication is replaced by simple addition.36 This means the model can compute complex expert routing decisions utilizing purely algebraic tropical addition and tropical multiplication. This mathematical reality perfectly aligns with QMINIWASM's multiplier-less WASM-based execution engine, entirely removing the need for computationally expensive softmax normalizations at the routing layer and allowing for hyper-fast, discrete token distribution.

### **Pillar 4: Quantum Information and Clifford Simulation**

The hybrid quantum-classical computing pillar of QMINIWASM represents its most experimental and highly specialized domain. Because a 1.58-bit ternary model possesses significantly less continuous representation capacity than a full-precision model, the architecture compensates by injecting high-dimensional nonlinearities derived from quantum state simulations into the classical network. Because universal quantum simulation is exponentially expensive and impossible on edge devices, the architecture relies heavily on the stabilizer formalism to maintain execution efficiency.

| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Sergey Bravyi** | IBM Quantum | Low-rank stabilizer decompositions; Magic State Injection; Gottesman-Knill Theorem applications | Hybrid Quantum-Classical Computing & Magic State Fidelity |

Sergey Bravyi is a preeminent figure in quantum information theory, specifically renowned for his monumental advancements in the classical simulation of quantum circuits and the application of the stabilizer formalism.39 According to the Gottesman-Knill theorem, quantum circuits that are strictly restricted to the Clifford group (operations that map Pauli matrices to other Pauli matrices) can be perfectly and efficiently simulated on a classical computer in polynomial time.44 However, Clifford circuits alone are not universal and cannot provide the complex, non-convex functional approximations required by the QMINIWASM routing matrices. To achieve quantum universality, non-Clifford elements—such as T-gates—must be explicitly introduced into the circuit via a complex process known as magic state injection.46

Bravyi's seminal research on simulating quantum circuits via low-rank stabilizer decompositions provides the exact mathematical framework needed for QMINIWASM's quantum-classical hybrid execution engine.39 By decomposing non-Clifford states into superpositions of highly optimized stabilizer states, Bravyi demonstrated that standard classical hardware can efficiently compute the expectation values of complex quantum subsystems without requiring exponential memory overhead.39

For the QMINIWASM architecture, which is designed to run on highly constrained classical WASM edge nodes 50, Bravyi's algorithms are revolutionary. They allow the architecture to mathematically simulate "magic states" and inject them directly into its discrete 1.58-bit computational graph. This mechanism—referred to as compute-uncompute quantum state fidelity—acts as an ultra-high-dimensional feature mapping.47 It allows the highly constrained ternary network to borrow the representational capacity of simulated quantum superpositions to solve complex non-convex optimization problems during edge inference. Without Bravyi's low-rank decomposition techniques, simulating these nonlinearities on an edge device would result in immediate out-of-memory errors, rendering the quantum-hybrid pillar of the architecture impossible.

## **WebAssembly (WASM) Edge Execution Integration**

The culmination of the datasets, the quantized training, the discrete RL optimization, and the tropical routing mechanics is ultimately channeled into QMINIWASM's execution environment: WebAssembly (WASM). WebAssembly is a binary instruction format for a stack-based virtual machine, designed as a portable compilation target for high-level languages.4 It has rapidly evolved beyond the browser into a universal execution platform for edge computing, IoT, and distributed infrastructure.50

The Bytecode Alliance, a leading consortium driving WASM standards, has pioneered runtimes such as Wasmtime and WasmEdge.4 These runtimes are explicitly designed to execute low-level binaries with near-native speed across diverse hardware architectures.55 QMINIWASM's 1.58-bit ternary architecture is perfectly symbiotic with this execution paradigm. Because WebAssembly relies heavily on efficient integer arithmetic and is often bottlenecked when processing complex floating-point SIMD (Single Instruction, Multiple Data) operations, a neural network architecture that entirely eliminates floating-point arithmetic natively excels in a WASM runtime.

By utilizing the continuous-to-discrete Cascade RL pipeline, QMINIWASM produces a final binary model that consists almost exclusively of integer additions and tropical geometry comparators. This model can be compiled directly into a highly compact WASM module.4 When deployed to an edge device running WasmEdge or Wasmtime, the architecture leverages WebAssembly's capability-based security model (WASI) to execute AI inferences without the overhead of massive GPU dependencies.4 The integration of execution-based code datasets like coding-eval 14 ensures the model understands this environment natively, while the simulated quantum magic states 39 provide the reasoning density required to perform complex logic locally.

## **Synthesis of the QMINIWASM Framework**

The theoretical viability of the QMINIWASM architecture rests squarely at the intersection of extreme computational minimization and high-dimensional geometric topology. By systematically replacing continuous, power-hungry representations with a 1.58-bit ternary lattice, the architecture successfully sidesteps the thermodynamic and von Neumann bottlenecks associated with floating-point memory bandwidth at the extreme edge.

The extensive curation of Hugging Face datasets provides the precise mathematical distributions required to survive this transition. The strict topological boundaries enforced by OpenMathInstruct-2 7 and the deterministic execution environments of xCodeEval 11 guarantee that the continuous-to-discrete phase transition occurs without catastrophic logic collapse. The Phase 1 mapping creates the necessary logical manifold, while Phase 3 distillation utilizes token-level KL-divergence derived from the dense synthetic reasoning traces of AM-DeepSeek-R1-Distilled 16 to embed deep reasoning directly into the ternary weights.

Simultaneously, the successful optimization of this discrete architecture relies entirely on the convergence of specialized theories from world-leading domain experts. Shuming Ma's BitLinear transforms 1 provide the physical memory mappings required for ternary addition, while Wei Ping's MOPD framework 33 dictates the precise distillation scaling laws needed to compress the teacher models. When the data is finally routed through the network, Ye Su and Yong Liu's discoveries in tropical geometry 36 translate continuous routing heuristics into algebraic hypersimplex mapping, completely bypassing non-differentiable bottlenecks. This routing is subsequently stabilized by Binyang Jiang's CISPO algorithm 23, ensuring variance-bounded updates in a fragile space. Finally, Sergey Bravyi's low-rank stabilizer decompositions 39 introduce quantum-level expressivity into the classically constrained edge device, recovering the representational capacity lost to quantization.

Together, these highly specialized datasets and advanced theoretical paradigms form the absolute foundation required to train, deploy, and scale the QMINIWASM extreme-edge AI framework across universal WebAssembly execution environments.
